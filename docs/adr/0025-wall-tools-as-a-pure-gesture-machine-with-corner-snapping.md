# ADR 0025: Wall tools as a pure gesture machine with grid-corner snapping; selection on the DM canvas; no wall undo yet

Status: accepted (T102). Amends ADR 0024 (the wall layer is interactive) and ADR 0022 (two more single-key shortcuts, `W` and `D`, and a new cheat-sheet group).

## Context
T101 gave the document walls and doors as free segments and the DM canvas a read-only picture of them. T102 has to let the DM draw a room in a handful of clicks on a printed map whose walls follow the grid — and just as often do not — then fix a corner or take a segment out. Every other canvas tool (alignment, fog, pen) is a pure state machine fed map-space pointer events by `DmCanvas`, and every edit to the document is one shared op; the wall tools should be no different, and the player must still never draw a wall.

## Decision
- **The wall and door tools are one pure state machine, `map/wallGesture.ts`,** `reduceWallGesture(state, event, config) → { state, segment? }`, like `fogGesture.ts` and `alignment.ts`. The **wall** tool is a polyline: a press places a vertex, every vertex after the first commits the segment from the previous one as its own `map/wall-set` at once, a release that travelled from its press places a vertex too (so a press-drag-release draws one segment), a double-click or Esc ends the polyline. Committing segment by segment rather than the whole polyline on finish means nothing is ever half-drawn: Esc always leaves a consistent map, the player has every finished segment immediately, and a crash mid-room loses at most the segment the pointer was still on. The **door** tool is press-drag-release for one closed door, because a door is one segment and a drag reads as "across the doorway". Rejected: one "wall" tool with a modifier for doors — a door is drawn rarely and deliberately, and a second button with its own key (`D`) is clearer than a held key.
- **Placed points snap to grid corners** — the lattice of `GridGeometry`, on or off the map (`grid.ts: nearestGridCorner`) — **when the point is within 12 screen pixels of one** (`WALL_SNAP_PX`, divided by the viewport scale), **and Shift skips snapping for that point.** A screen-pixel threshold feels the same at every zoom; snapping to corners, not to the nearest grid line, is what a room drawn on a gridded map wants, and Shift is the universal "exactly here" modifier for the diagonal wall or the mid-cell doorway. The preview shows the snapped point (a ring under the pointer), so what is previewed is what is committed. Rejected: snapping to arbitrary segment endpoints too — useful for T-junctions, but ambiguous next to a corner; a later task can add it as a second snap target.
- **The double-click rule accounts for the browser's own event order.** Konva reports a double-click on any release within 400 ms of the previous one, position aside; two quick clicks on two corners must stay two corners. So a double-click ends the polyline only when the press it came with placed no vertex (it landed on the last one), and the idle state remembers where it ended (`endedAt`) to swallow the press-and-release the browser sends after the double-click event, which would otherwise start a stray one-vertex polyline. The one edge left — a new polyline started within 400 ms of a double-click on a *different* spot gets a phantom double-click — is kept as a new start, never swallowed.
- **Selection and editing happen on the canvas, with no tool armed,** the way tokens do: a click on a segment selects it (the DM wall layer's nodes listen, generously, only while no tool is armed and Space is up), the selected one shows a halo and two draggable end handles, a drop is one `map/wall-set` of the patched segment (snapped the same way, Shift skipping it), Delete / Backspace or the panel's button is one `map/wall-remove`, Esc deselects. The selection is DM UI state (`DmView`), cleared by a map switch, by the segment's removal and by arming any tool. Rejected: a list of segments in the panel to select from — segments have no names, and pointing at the one on the map is the only way a DM identifies it; T103's door list is a different matter (doors are few and have a state).
- **Wall edits are not undoable.** The fog history (ADR 0013) is a redo stack over the fog log only; extending it to walls means a general document history, which is worth its own design. Until then the guide says so and the tools make every step cheap to redo by hand (delete, redraw, drag an end).
- **The DM surface reports the segments** (`data-wall-segments`, `kind:x1,y1,x2,y2;…`) next to their count, so the e2e can assert snapped coordinates without reading the store; **the player view reports only the count** (`data-walls`), which keeps `src/player/` free of any endpoint read (ADR 0024's boundary test) while still letting the e2e see the mirror follow.

## Consequences
- `StagePointerTool` handlers receive the event's modifiers (`{ shift }`) and may take `onDoubleClick`; `MapStage` forwards Konva's `dblclick` / `dbltap`. Existing tools ignore both.
- `DmTool` gains `wall`; the chip says `Walls · wall` / `Walls · door`; `SHORTCUTS` gains `W` / `D` in a **Walls** group and `MOUSE_GESTURES` the selection click; the guide's keyboard table and the cheat sheet follow from the tables (21 shortcut rows).
- `dm/WallLayer.tsx` grows the interactive half (hit width, halo, handles, live redraw of a dragged end); its read-only rendering is unchanged, and `player/boundary.test.ts` still keeps it out of `map/` and `player/`.
- Konva redraws a layer's hit graph on the next animation frame after its nodes start listening: a click inside that frame after putting a tool down misses. No human clicks that fast; the e2e waits a frame.
- T103 adds the door click over the same interactive layer; a document history for walls (and tokens, notes) is a candidate for a later task.
